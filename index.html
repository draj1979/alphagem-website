The problem

Retail investors are asked to decide with the least reliable information.

Professionals have analysts, terminals and hours to read filings. Most individual investors have a phone, a few apps and a group chat.

Information is scattered

Results sit in exchange filings, guidance in call transcripts, ownership in shareholding patterns, news somewhere else. Stitching it together takes hours per company, so most people don't.

Headline numbers can describe the wrong company

Many data tools show the parent company only. For a group with large subsidiaries, scale, growth, profit and the P/E can look very different once you see the consolidated figures.

The real signal is buried in long documents

What management expects next, what they blamed for a weak quarter, and which risks they raised are in 40-page transcripts and slide decks that few investors have time to read.

Tips, hype and unsourced claims

Social feeds and messaging groups reward confidence, not accuracy. A bold target with no source, date or reasoning is hard to tell apart from a considered view.

Stale or undated data

A figure without a period or filing date can be months old, restated, or from a different basis. Without that context it is easy to compare things that are not comparable.

AI that sounds sure, even when it isn't

General chatbots can invent figures or quotes and present them fluently. For money decisions, an answer you cannot verify is a liability, not a shortcut.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Is AlphaGem investment advice?

No. AlphaGem produces research information from public disclosures and data. It does not give price targets or buy, hold or sell recommendations. Please read our disclaimer and consider speaking to a SEBI-registered adviser before you invest.

What do I get when I register?

100 free credits, which you can use to generate reports. That is roughly 5 to 10 reports, depending on how many credits each one uses.

How many credits does a report use?

Between 10 and 20 credits. The exact number depends on how much the AI (Gemini) reads and writes for that report, so it varies with how much source material the company has.

Which companies can I research?

NSE-listed companies. Depth varies by company. Where data or documents are missing, the report says exactly what is missing instead of guessing.

Where does the information come from?

Exchange filings and announcements, company-published documents such as earnings-call transcripts and investor presentations, licensed financial data, and market prices. Every report lists its sources with dates.

Can the AI make mistakes?

Yes. That is why AlphaGem labels AI-written text, requires it to cite evidence, checks quotes word-for-word against the filing and verifies figures. Please still check the linked sources before relying on anything.

Do I need a card to start?

No. Registration gives you the free credits. You only pay if you decide to buy more.
